Why we're doing this

We can both be trying hard and still keep missing each other, because each of us is working from our own list of what love looks like. I've put effort into the things that would make me feel loved, then been puzzled when they didn't land for you. This is how we stop guessing. Every two weeks, each of us says what we need and how it's going. It isn't about keeping score. The point is that asking becomes an ordinary part of us.

I recognized us in that. More than that, I recognized myself. I've put a lot of effort into the things that would make me feel loved, and then been puzzled when it didn't seem to land for you. You didn't ask me to read it so I could review it. You asked because there's something you need me to understand.

This is me asking you to tell me what that is.

I know the timing is strange. Nothing about right now is a fair picture of us, and some of these numbers are going to be low. Put them down low anyway. A flattering answer today is no use to either of us.

The book calls it a love bank. Every ordinary exchange puts love units in or takes them out, and being in love is mostly the balance being high. A balance is something you can watch move, so I'd like us to do this often rather than once. Not to keep score. So that asking what you need becomes an ordinary part of us rather than a thing I did one time.

Thank you for asking me to read it. I know that wasn't a small thing to ask.

    The ten needs come from His Needs, Her Needs by Willard F. Harley Jr. The descriptions and the balance formula are ours: for each need, how it's going × how much it matters, as a share of the most it could be.
